# Notebooks: evidence for pipeline decisions

Notebooks here are **EDA and design validation only**. Production code lives in `src/gps/` and never imports from notebooks. Each notebook answers one question, and every threshold in `src/` cites the notebook that justifies it. The numbering follows the dependency order of the decisions: for example, the 1100 km/h threshold from 01 is used by the duplicate rule in 02.

| # | Notebook | Question | Decision | Used in `src/` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | `01_speed_threshold` | What speed between two consecutive points is physically impossible? | `impossible_speed_kmh = 1100`, between the fastest verified real point (airplane, 1,048.1 km/h) and the slowest verified error (1,122.7 km/h). The 180 km/h drift filter was dropped. Section 6: GPS noise ≈ 34 m; noise-corrected labelled speeds match real-world speeds (web sources); per-mode limits rejected (1.2–2.3% false rejection of real points). Airliners with tailwind can exceed 1100 km/h (up to 1,327), not seen in GeoLife. | `CleaningThresholds.impossible_speed_kmh` |
| 02 | `02_duplicate_timestamps` | Several records share a timestamp: which one to keep? | Validate candidates against the nearest non-duplicate anchors at ≤ 1100 km/h. Quarantine a group when no candidate is valid, or when ≥ 2 valid candidates are > 200 m apart ("ambiguous"). Part 3: keep the candidate with the lowest `max(v_in, v_out)` instead of file order (no randomness; pick moves a median 0.7 m), separate `invalid_anchors` reason; after spike removal those groups are re-checked with new anchors (13/16 rescued). | `_resolve_duplicate_groups`, `recheck_invalid_anchor_groups` |
| 03 | `03_segment_gap_threshold` | After how long a gap should a new segment start? | `max_gap_seconds = 1200`, inside the within-trip gap P99.9 (252 s) – P99.99 (2,170 s). Gaps cannot detect trip boundaries. | `CleaningThresholds.max_gap_seconds`, `segment_trajectories` |
| 04 | `04_file_boundaries` | Should stay-point detection run per file, on all files concatenated, or on chained files? | Chain consecutive files when the gap between them is ≤ 18 h, the point where "resuming within 200 m" falls to the level of gaps > 24 h. Per-file detection misses overnight stays (395 → 3,340 night stays; users without a night stay 96 → 41). | `StayPointDetector.detect(max_gap_hours=MAX_GAP_HOURS)` (since cut at any gap > 18 h) |
| 05 | `05_staypoint_cleaning_impact` | How does each cleaning step change the detected stay-points? | Ablation on all 182 users. Chaining files (≤ 18 h) instead of joining all files is the decisive step: 19,443 → 18,598 stay-points, longest 1,459 days → 43.8 h, 282 stays > 48 h → 0. Duplicate resolution changes 0.8% of stay-points (136/152 by a median 1.3 m; the large changes are mostly stays re-joined once an ambiguous group is quarantined). Spike removal changes none (no spike within 30 min of a stay). No cleaning step removes a real stay. Side finding: 7 users never have a stay-point; some `.plt` files are byte-identical across users. | None (validation of `clean_trajectory` + `detect`) |
| 06 | `06_gaps_inside_staypoints` | Should a stay-point be split at a long unobserved gap inside it? | No: device resumes a few tens of metres away and labelled trips fall inside such gaps in only 0.2–1.9% of cases. Store `observed_minutes` alongside `duration_minutes`. | `StayPoint.observed_minutes` |
| 07 | `07_staypoint_thresholds` | Are 200 m / 30 min the right stay-point thresholds? Should CENTROID windows be trimmed of their approach/leave tails? | Keep both. Negative control = vehicle trips ≤ 3 h, positive control = stops between labelled trips. 30 min is the largest value that still finds 30–45 min stops (53% → 33% at 45 min), with 3.5 false stays per 100 vehicle-hours (102 at 5 min). 200 m covers the stationary footprint (median P90 136 m); above it recall and false stays both grow linearly, no clear optimum. Tails: do not trim — trimming moves the centroid a median 14 m and times by < 0.3 min, recall −0.3 pt, false stays −5%. | `StayPointDetector` defaults |
| 08 | `08_trajectory_jumps` | Do multi-point excursions and one-sided jumps (> 1100 km/h, not single spikes) need a cleaning rule? | No new rule. 58 excursions touch 11 stay-points (0.03% of dwell) and the "familiar place" test cannot tell which side is wrong (24% vs 26%). 250 one-sided jumps (median 1.8 km in 4 s, both sides mostly familiar) look like timing glitches at signal re-acquisition; stays cannot straddle them. Points are kept and the segment is split. | `segment_trajectories` (unchanged) |
| 09 | `09_timezone_by_location` | Is a fixed UTC+8 correct for every user? | No: 2.61% of points (16 users, 2 mostly abroad) have another UTC offset; under fixed UTC+8 21.5% of their active hours fall at 01–05 h vs 7.4% by location (reference 5.7%). Use the IANA timezone of the coordinates (`timezonefinder` + `zoneinfo`, DST aware). | `gps.data.timezone.localize_by_location` (`tz_name`, `timestamp_local`) |
| 10 | `10_staypoint_validation` | Are the detected stay-points correct? (no home labels exist, so: tier 3 = required properties on all data; tier 4 = blind map audit of a stratified sample) | All 18,597 stays of 174 users satisfy all 9 properties (bounds are real points, count, centroid, ≥ 30 min, observed time, no gap > 18 h, running-centroid ≤ 200 m, window maximal, no overlap). Mutation control: direct corruptions caught 100%, wrong-parameter detectors 86–100%; the rest are valid windows with another start, i.e. the properties check validity, not missed stays (notebook 07 covers recall). Tier 4 (60 stays, one grader): precision 92% (95% CI 75–98%; 77% if "unsure" counts as wrong), 90.6% of sampled dwell time correct; the only error type is slow movement along a road (30–45 min); long / sparse / overnight stays 17/18 correct, 0 wrong. | `test_stay_points_satisfy_detector_definition` |
| 11 | `11_shared_recordings` | Why are some `.plt` files byte-identical across users, and does it matter? (documentation only; users stay independent) | 821 contents / 52 users / 44 pairs, 12% of clean points; 11 users are 100% shared (057, 094, 150 have identical datasets). Mostly real joint activity: shared files are at the members' own places (75.5% vs 23.4% for strangers), unfamiliar ones are long trips (median 807 km away, 4.9 h), and pairs with shared files are co-located on separate devices far more often (39% vs 1.1% of pairs). Risks for later: double counting and train/test leakage across users who share files; group them when evaluating. | None (users stay independent) |
| 12 | `12_home_office_time_windows` | Should home/office be scored by arrival hour or by minutes inside the window, and which windows? | Score each stay by its minutes inside the local-time window: home found for 164/174 users (130 by arrival hour), agreement with the core night place 62% → 69%, office 50% → 66%. Windows read from the hourly profile of the night/day place: home 22–08 h (every day), office 09–18 h Mon–Fri (the day place leads only on weekdays). Window edges move the result less than its noise (home 62–72%, office 64–70%): reasonable, not optimal. Per-stay scoring caps at ~70% because full nights tie (76/164 users); scoring a 200 m area gives 89%/84% and confidence 0.18 → 0.69, the case for v2. Rule "office ≠ home stay" lowers agreement (66 → 63%), not adopted. | `HeuristicClassifier` (`home_hour_*`, `office_hour_*`, `work_days`) |
| 13 | `13_minimum_evidence` | When should v1 answer "unknown" instead of a home/office? | 14 | `14_place_clustering` | v2: how should DBSCAN group stays into places (`eps`, `min_samples`, count stays or distinct days), and when should v2 answer "unknown"? (split-half stability as in 13, plus place width and home/office merged into one cluster as a chaining check) | Stability is flat from `eps` 100 m up (55–68%, CIs overlap); larger `eps` widens places (P90 radius 275 → 949 m) and merges home with office beyond the real co-location rate (26–28%) from 300 m. Reasonable, not optimal. | `ClusterClassifier`, `place_labels`, `clustering.eps_meters` / `min_samples` / `min_window_days` |

## Conventions

- **Language:** narrative in Vietnamese (markdown cells); code, printed output, file names, figure titles, axis labels and table columns in English.
- **Outputs:** everything a notebook writes goes to `outputs/<notebook>/`:
  - `figures/`: `figNN_<what_it_shows>.png`, committed.
  - `tables/`: small CSVs with the evidence and decisions, committed.
  - `cache/`: expensive intermediate results (parquet); git-ignored and regenerated when missing.
  - `maps/`: interactive folium HTML maps; git-ignored.
- **Data:** notebooks read raw GeoLife from `../data/raw/` and the cleaned output from `../data/processed/`. They may import from `../src` to measure the production pipeline's behaviour; a design not yet in `src/` is prototyped inside the notebook itself.
- **Running:** use the `gps` conda env. The first run of a notebook fills its `cache/` and can take several minutes; later runs reuse the cache. Delete the cache to recompute on new data.
